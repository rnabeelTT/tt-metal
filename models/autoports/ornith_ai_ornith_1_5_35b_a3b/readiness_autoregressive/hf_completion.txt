Confused, she asked her grandfather, an old astronomer who had spent his life studying the stars. He smiled and said, "The sun doesn't move, my dear. It's the Earth that spins. But let me teach you how to understand the sky — not just what you see, but why."

He began by drawing a circle in the dirt — a great wheel he called the celestial sphere. "Imagine the entire sky is a giant ball surrounding us," he explained. "The stars are fixed on its inner surface. As the Earth turns, this ball appears to rotate around us."

Elena watched as he marked two special points: the North Celestial Pole, directly above the North Pole, and the celestial equator, a line halfway between the poles. "Now," he said, "the key to understanding the sky is knowing your latitude — how far north or south you are."
